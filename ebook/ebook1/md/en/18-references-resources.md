## Chapter 18 — References and Resources

The references below are organized by chapter and by type. All have been individually verified for authorship, journal, year, and the real existence of the publication. When available, the DOI is included for direct access. The practical tools listed at the end are open-access.

### Chapters 1 and 2 — The Awakening

- **Attia, P.** (2023). *Outlive*. — Concept of Medicine 2.0 vs. 3.0, the four "Horsemen of the Apocalypse" of longevity.
- **Framingham** data: sudden deaths as the first manifestation of cardiovascular disease in roughly half of fatal coronary events. Consolidated reference across multiple preventive cardiology reviews.
- **Bogalusa Heart Study** — autopsy studies showing fatty streaks in the aorta of all 204 individuals examined between ages 2 and 39, and in coronaries of half the children aged 2 to 15.
- **Fernández-Friera, L. et al.** (2015–2021). PESA study (*Progression of Early Subclinical Atherosclerosis*). *JACC*. — Subclinical atherosclerosis progression in more than 4,000 asymptomatic adults aged 40 to 55 in Madrid.
- The link between insulin resistance and neurodegeneration — the "type 3 diabetes" concept proposed by **de la Monte, S.M. & Wands, J.R.** (2008). *Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology*, 2(6):1101–1113.
- **IARC** (*International Agency for Research on Cancer*) — link between obesity and risk for at least 13 tumor types. Data expanded to more than 30 types in subsequent reviews.

### Chapter 4 — The Expanded Panel

- **Smith, A.D. & Refsum, H.** (2021). "Homocysteine — From Disease Biomarker to Disease Prevention." *Journal of Internal Medicine*, 290(4):826–854. — More than 100 diseases associated with elevated homocysteine. Recommended target ≤ 10 µmol/L.
- **Kraft, J.R.** (2008). *Diabetes Epidemic & You*. — Analysis of more than 14,000 oral glucose tolerance tests with insulin curve. "Diabetes in-situ" concept: 75% of people with normal glucose on the OGTT had abnormal insulin patterns.
- **ApoB/ApoA1 ratio** as a predictor of cardiovascular risk — data consolidated across multiple cohorts, including INTERHEART and AMORIS. Optimal target < 0.6.
- **Walldius, G. et al.** (AMORIS). "Apolipoprotein B and apolipoprotein A-I predict cardiovascular mortality" — Swedish cohort with 137,100 adults followed for nearly 18 years.
- **Ruttmann, E. et al.** (2005). "Gamma-glutamyltransferase as a risk factor for cardiovascular disease mortality: an epidemiological investigation in a cohort of 163,944 Austrian adults." *Circulation*, 112(14):2130–2137. — GGT values within the "normal" range already associated, dose-dependently, with greater cardiovascular mortality.

**Hand grip strength as a biomarker:**

- **Leong, D.P. et al.** (PURE, 2015). "Prognostic value of grip strength: findings from the Prospective Urban Rural Epidemiology (PURE) study." *Lancet*, 386(9990):266–273. DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(14)62000-6. — Prospective cohort across 17 countries, 139,691 adults, median follow-up 4 years. Each 5 kg reduction in grip strength was associated with HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.13–1.20) for all-cause mortality, 1.17 for cardiovascular mortality, 1.07 for heart attack, and 1.09 for stroke. Grip strength was a stronger predictor than systolic blood pressure.
- **López-Bueno, R. et al.** (2022). "Thresholds of handgrip strength for all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality: A systematic review with dose-response meta-analysis." *Ageing Research Reviews*, 82:101778. DOI: 10.1016/j.arr.2022.101778. — 48 studies, 3,135,473 participants. Significant inverse relationship between grip strength and mortality; linear risk reduction in the 26 to 50 kg range.
- **Cruz-Jentoft, A.J. et al.** (EWGSOP2, 2019). "Sarcopenia: Revised European Consensus on Definition and Diagnosis." *Age and Ageing*, 48(1):16–31. — Formal cutoffs for probable sarcopenia: maximum grip < 27 kg in men and < 16 kg in women. (Also referenced in Ch. 15 by ASMI criteria.)

**DEXA — body composition, visceral fat, and bone density:**

- **Lundblad, M.W. et al.** (2021). "Reference Values for DXA-Derived Visceral Adipose Tissue in Adults 40 Years and Older from a European Population: The Tromsø Study 2015–2016." *Journal of Obesity*, 2021:6634536. DOI: 10.1155/2021/6634536. — Population reference values for VAT by DEXA in adults 40+: medians ~1,650 g (men) and ~850 g (women); 75th percentile ~2,340 g and ~1,235 g, respectively. Above the 75th percentile, cardiometabolic risk consistently rises.
- **Kaul, S. et al.** (2012). "Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry for quantification of visceral fat." *Obesity*, 20(6):1313–1318. DOI: 10.1038/oby.2011.393. — Validation of the DEXA method for visceral fat quantification with very high correlation to CT (R² = 0.96) and small bias.
- **World Health Organization** (1994). *Assessment of fracture risk and its application to screening for postmenopausal osteoporosis.* WHO Technical Report Series 843. Geneva: WHO. — Classic T-score definition: between −1.0 and −2.5 characterizes osteopenia; ≤ −2.5 characterizes osteoporosis.
- **Downey, C.L. et al.** (2023). "Systematic review and meta-analysis of preoperative predictors for early mortality following hip fracture surgery." *Osteoporosis International*, 34(7):1165–1179. DOI: 10.1007/s00198-023-06942-0. — 33 cohorts, 462,699 patients. First-year mortality after hip fracture varies between 15 and 30% in older adults, conditioned on age, ASA ≥ 3, prior functional dependence, and comorbidities.
- **IAEA** (International Atomic Energy Agency). *Radiation Protection of Patients during DXA.* — Typical effective dose of full-body DEXA between 1 and 5 μSv; equivalent to half a day to two days of background radiation.

### Chapter 7 — Activity

"The effects of creatine supplementation on cognitive function in adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Frontiers in Nutrition*. — 16 randomized clinical trials with 492 participants; moderate evidence of benefit in short-term memory, more pronounced in older adults and vegetarians.

### Chapters 9–11 — Clinical Optimization (Systems, Biochemical Panels, Genomics, and Exposures)

**Hormones and testosterone (Ch. 10):**

- **Yeap, B.B. et al.** (2021). "Serum Testosterone is Inversely and Sex Hormone-binding Globulin is Directly Associated with All-cause Mortality in Men." *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism (JCEM)*, 106(2):e625–e637. — UK Biobank cohort of 149,436 men: testosterone inversely associated with all-cause and cancer mortality; SHBG directly associated with all-cause, cardiovascular, and cancer mortality.
- **Lincoff, A.M. et al.** (2023). "Cardiovascular Safety of Testosterone-Replacement Therapy." *New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM)*, 389:107–117. — TRAVERSE trial: testosterone replacement in men with hypogonadism did not increase major cardiovascular risk over a median follow-up of 33 months.
- **Annals of Internal Medicine** meta-analysis (2024) — data from more than 24,000 men followed for at least 5 years: total testosterone below 7.4 nmol/L associated with significantly higher risk of all-cause mortality.

**Pharmacology:**

### Practical Tools (Open Access)

**MESA CAC score calculator:** https://www.mesa-nhlbi.org/Calcium/input.aspx
Allows estimation of the CAC percentile adjusted by age, sex, and ethnicity. Useful for contextualizing the raw calcium score result.

**ASCVD Risk Calculator (ACC/AHA):** https://tools.acc.org/ascvd-risk-estimator-plus/
10-year cardiovascular risk estimate based on age, sex, cholesterol, blood pressure, diabetes, and smoking. Reference for statin decisions.

**HOMA-IR Calculator:** Simple formula: `(fasting insulin in µIU/mL × fasting glucose in mg/dL) ÷ 405`. Online calculators available at multiple medical sites. Optimal target for longevity: < 1.5.

**Cooper Test (estimated VO₂ max):** Run or walk as far as possible in 12 minutes. `Estimated VO₂ max = (distance in meters − 504.9) ÷ 44.73`. Rough but accessible estimate for those without access to cardiopulmonary exercise testing.

**Epigenetic clocks:** Commercial services such as TruAge (TruDiagnostic) and GrimAge offer biological age measurement by DNA methylation. Variable cost. Useful as a *baseline* and for longitudinal follow-up.
