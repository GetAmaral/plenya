# PART III — The ACTS Method

Across the previous six chapters, we built the map: what is killing people after 40, how the body ages on the inside, which numbers really matter, what the arteries and the metabolism reveal once you know where to look. From here on, the book turns from diagnosis to action.

Over years of clinical practice, I organized the interventions I prescribe into four pillars — and noticed that, together, they formed an acronym summarizing exactly what I ask of every patient. In Portuguese the method is called **AGIR**, which means *to act*. In English, the equivalent acronym is **ACTS** — the same imperative, the same call to action.

**A — Activity (Exercise & Movement), Alimentation (Eating Well) & Smart Adjuncts.** The three engines of lifestyle that no medication can replace — and the visible layer (skin, hair, nails) that translates, on the outside, the health built on the inside. By **activity** I mean both formal training and the unstructured movement that fills the day. By **alimentation** I mean the deliberate, ongoing act of eating well, broader than any single diet. By **smart adjuncts** I mean supplements and compounds chosen for what bloodwork actually says you need, not what marketing sells. Chapters 7 and 8.

**C — Clinical Optimization (Systems, Panels & Exposures).** Three layers read as one. **Systems** — the cardio-renal-hepatic-metabolic network treated as a single organism (Ch 9). **Panels** — the biochemistry and hormones that whisper before the symptom shouts (Ch 10). **Exposures** — genomics, epigenetics, and the silent environmental exposures the body carries (Ch 11).

**T — Tending Mind, Body & Bonds (Inner Work + Connection).** Two layers the standard checkup never asks about. The first (Ch 12) is the inner work — psychoneuroimmunology, trauma, psychological assessment, nervous-system regulation, cognitive function. The second (Ch 13) is the relational and existential dimension — sexual life, social bonds, screens, purpose, spirituality, and the bidirectional loop between anxiety and inflammation. Ana opens the first; Ricardo (the patient from Chapter 1), now post-heart-attack, opens the second.

**S — Sleep, Rhythm & Recovery.** Sleep, chronobiology, and the internal clocks that synchronize everything — from insulin sensitivity to memory consolidation. **Sleep** is architecture, depth, and apnea. **Rhythm** is circadian timing of food, light, and movement. **Recovery** is the active rest between sessions, weeks, and seasons of life. Chapter 14.

The four pillars do not work in isolation. Alimentation without activity is incomplete. Activity without sleep is overtraining. Hormonal optimization without mental health is building on sand. The strength of the method is in the integration — and in personalizing each pillar according to what each patient's biomarkers demand.

> *AGIR means "to act" in Portuguese. ACTS is its English counterpart — four pillars that, together, protect the decades of health that standard medicine gives up on.*

> We begin with the pillar that depends most on you.
